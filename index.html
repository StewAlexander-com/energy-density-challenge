Start with the outcome

What do people need, and what limits it?

Energy density means energy stored per unit of volume. Energy per unit of mass is called specific energy. Either can matter when space or weight is a limit.

Read the full research question

Can human and AI research teams find ways to deliver more useful energy with fewer resource demands and lower economic and environmental costs?

Or would the apparent gain shift energy use, cost, risk or environmental damage somewhere else?

The methodology sets out the comparison rules. The challenge definition (JSON) preserves the formal question for research systems.

The first study

Give density a fair chance to lose.

Choose one real use case. Compare better energy storage with practical alternatives, such as efficiency or infrastructure improvements. Decide in advance what would count as a meaningful benefit.

Then follow the full costs and harms. The result may be to keep, narrow or replace the question.

Read the plan for the first study
  1. Define the outcome. Who needs it, where and when?
  2. Compare the options. What is limiting progress today?
  3. Test what would change the decision. Include uncertainty and costs beyond the device.
