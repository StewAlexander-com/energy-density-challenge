DO NOT REGRESS

Preserve the epistemic improvements already introduced in State 0001.

In particular, do not regress to:

1. Energy density as the predetermined objective

Energy density remains a candidate variable and potentially important systems leverage point.

It must compete against alternatives.

2. Network centrality as proof of causal leverage

A systems map can nominate candidates.

It cannot establish winners.

3. Multi-AI agreement as independent evidence

Different models may share training sources, assumptions, cultural priors, architectures, benchmarks, and failure modes.

Agreement is evidence about agreement.

It is not automatically evidence about reality.

4. “Entropy” as a metaphor for every displaced burden

Retain the scientifically precise distinction between thermodynamic entropy and broader displaced burdens.

Continue asking:

Where did the entropy go?

where thermodynamically appropriate.

Also ask:

Where did the burdens go?

for costs displaced into materials, mining, manufacturing, toxicity, infrastructure, land, water, emissions, geopolitics, labor, safety, waste, or other systems.

5. Confidence as truth

Confidence remains conditional on assumptions, evidence, methodology, system boundaries, and current knowledge.

6. Immutability as truth

Cryptographic integrity establishes that a research state has not been silently altered.

It does not establish that the research state is correct.

7. Endless deliberation as intelligence

More inference is not necessarily more knowledge.

The protocol must be capable of deciding that the next rational action is an observation, experiment, literature search, human consultation, engineering prototype—or no action at all.

⸻

THE NEW RESEARCH OBJECT

Investigate whether we can construct a scientific intelligence-allocation protocol for energy research.

Its purpose would be to answer recursively:

Given what is currently known, what is the highest-value next use of intelligence, measurement, experimentation, or resources?

That may mean:

* thinking more,
* asking another AI,
* asking a human expert,
* searching prior art,
* collecting new data,
* constructing a simulation,
* performing a calculation,
* building a prototype,
* running an experiment,
* replicating an experiment,
* challenging a system boundary,
* searching for displaced burdens,
* changing the objective,
* abandoning a hypothesis,
* or stopping until new information becomes available.

The protocol should not privilege computation merely because AI computation is available.

INTELLIGENCE HAS A COST

Treat intelligence itself as a constrained resource.

Every research action consumes some combination of:

* inference,
* compute,
* electricity,
* human attention,
* laboratory time,
* equipment,
* materials,
* money,
* opportunity cost,
* elapsed time,
* and environmental resources.

Therefore ask:

What research action produces the greatest expected reduction in decision-relevant uncertainty per constrained resource?

Do not assume this exact formulation is optimal.

Critique and improve it if necessary.

A rational protocol should not add it merely to increase the number of agents.

Suppose two hypotheses remain unresolved after extensive reasoning but predict measurably different outcomes under one inexpensive test.

Stop debating.

Preserve the disagreement and identify the missing capability rather than manufacturing consensus.

MAKE ENERGY DENSITY COMPETE

Energy density should remain an important founding hypothesis.

Do not discard it merely because its privileged status has been removed.

Instead, make it compete fairly.

Across many energy-service problems, determine whether energy-density interventions repeatedly dominate or materially improve the Pareto frontier.

If they do, the founding hypothesis becomes more strongly supported.

If energy density dominates only certain domains, identify those domains.

If another variable repeatedly dominates, follow that evidence.

If there is no universal leverage variable, investigate whether the energy system instead contains a topology of constraints:

Different useful services may be bottlenecked by different physical, economic, infrastructural, environmental, or informational constraints.

That possibility should itself be tested rather than assumed.

MULTIPLE INTELLIGENCES WITHOUT ARTIFICIAL CONSENSUS

Different intelligences should initially investigate important questions independently where feasible.

Do not immediately expose every participant to every other participant’s reasoning.

Premature sharing can create correlated reasoning and convergence around an attractive but incorrect framing.

Preserve independent branches.

Later compare them.

Record:

* independent convergence,
* disagreement,
* unique hypotheses,
* shared assumptions,
* likely correlated sources,
* contradictory predictions,
* confidence changes,
* and what evidence could discriminate between competing positions.

The objective is not consensus.

The objective is better-calibrated belief and better research decisions.

⸻

REALITY IS THE ARBITER

No AI receives epistemic authority merely because it is powerful.

No human receives epistemic authority merely because they originated the project.

No committee receives epistemic authority merely because its members agree.

No Merkle root establishes scientific truth.

No citation count establishes scientific truth.

No simulation establishes physical truth outside its assumptions.

No confidence score establishes truth.

When competing explanations make distinguishable predictions, prioritize contact with reality.

Measurement, reproducibility, experiment, observation, and successful prediction should progressively constrain the research graph.

PRESERVE DISAGREEMENT

Do not average incompatible hypotheses into artificial compromise.

Create branches.

For example:

HYPOTHESIS A
      │
      ├── prediction
      ├── evidence
      └── assumptions
HYPOTHESIS B
      │
      ├── different prediction
      ├── evidence
      └── assumptions
            ↓
   DISCRIMINATING TEST
            ↓
evidence updates both branches

A branch that loses support should remain historically accessible.

A falsified idea is research output.

A null result is research output.

A failed experiment is research output.

A disagreement that cannot yet be resolved is research output.
